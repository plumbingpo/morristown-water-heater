A busy household runs through a surprising amount of hot water every day, and a struggling water heater disrupts everything from morning showers to the evening dishes. Left alone, a heavy layer of sediment or a weeping relief valve can turn an inconvenience into real water damage. Clear Pricing

Honest, Upfront Pricing

Nobody likes a surprise on the invoice. That's why Morristown Water Heater prices every job upfront, after a full diagnostic that looks at the heater's age, fuel type, and capacity, the burner assembly or heating elements, the anode rod and relief valve, and the venting and supply lines. You'll know the problem, what the fix involves, and exactly what it will cost before you say yes.

The problem with water heater failures is that many of them develop inside the tank where you can't see them. Minerals settle into a crust on the bottom of the tank, the anode rod wears down a little at a time, and once it's gone, the tank itself starts to corrode. If the water turns rusty, the tank rumbles, or you spot moisture under the heater, have it checked before a repair turns into a replacement.

How old is your water heater? The serial number on the label usually tells us. Once a tank passes the 10-year mark, it's worth planning a replacement before the current one decides for you. As a rough guide, a 40 to 50 gallon gas tank typically runs $1,400–$2,800 installed, and an electric tank typically runs $1,100–$2,400, depending on venting, code updates, and access. Our team will tell you plainly whether yours has years left or is nearing the end.

Maintenance Plans

Water Heater Maintenance That Adds Years

Preventing a breakdown beats paying for one. That's the whole point of our maintenance plans. Once a year, we flush the sediment out of the tank, check the anode rod, exercise the relief valve, look over the venting and gas connections or the elements and wiring, and descale tankless heat exchangers, all matched to the equipment in your home.

Many homes do best with one flush a year, and homes with hard water often need a visit every six months. The schedule flexes with the age of your heater, how hard your household works it, and the mineral content of your water, which controls how quickly sediment builds up and how long an anode rod lasts.

The savings show up faster than most people expect. A tank without a sediment crust heats water more efficiently, a replaced anode rod can add years to the tank, and a tested relief valve means the tank can safely vent pressure if something goes wrong. Compared with the cost of an early replacement, a maintenance plan is a bargain.

Sized for Your Household

A Water Heater Sized for Your Actual Household

When it comes to water heaters, bigger is not automatically better. What matters for a tank is the first-hour rating, how many gallons of hot water it can deliver in one busy hour, while for a tankless unit it's the flow rate it can hold when the incoming water is cold. That's why we never just copy the gallon rating of the old unit. Instead, we look at how many people live in the home, the number of bathrooms, plus large draws such as a jetted tub or high-capacity washer.

We walk you through the options for every home, especially growing families with rising hot water demands. A 40-gallon tank that served a couple rarely holds out after two teenagers start showering every morning, and a tankless water heater can deliver steady hot water so long as it's sized for the flow. If you're weighing tank against tankless, or gas against a heat pump model, our technician will lay out the honest trade-offs: installed price, operating cost, gas line and venting requirements, electrical capacity, and space, so you can pick what fits your home and budget.

Common Problems

Hot Water Problems We Solve Every Week

The majority of service calls we take come down to a handful of familiar problems. The water never gets hot. The pilot lights and then goes out a minute later. The shower goes cold after a few minutes, the tank rumbles and pops, or the tankless unit locks out with an error code. Rusty water, a rotten-egg smell, and a dripping relief valve round out the list. We fix every one of them, starting with the exact part that failed.

Some problems are just a nuisance, while others pose real risks. A burned-out element simply leaves you with lukewarm water, but a relief valve that keeps discharging is warning you that temperature or pressure inside the tank is too high, and a thermostat set above 120°F can burn a child in seconds. If you ever smell gas near the heater, leave the house and call your gas utility from outside before you call us. We match the fix to the failure: elements and thermostats on electric tanks, thermocouples, flame sensors, and gas valves on gas units, expansion tanks where water pressure is spiking, and leak repair on fittings, drain valves, and relief lines.
